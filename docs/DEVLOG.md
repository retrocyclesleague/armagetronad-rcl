## 2026-10-19 - render-next P3: the D2 light cycle, shaded like the render

Jamie picked the D2 design. `RCL_CYCLE_MODEL 2` draws it with the new renderer (`gCycleMesh.cpp`, `shaders/cycle_pbr.*`); the arrow (0) stays the default.

- **Where it stands:** in the light cycle's frame (`gLightCycle`), scaled uniformly to its length (3.02 in the frame, 1.51 in the arena) with the nose at the light cycle's nose, so the tail the wall leaves is where it was. D2 is wider than the light cycle at that length: 0.99 across in the frame (0.49 in the arena) against 0.68; the arrow is 1.64. It leans with the cycle; its wheels turn by the way come over their own radii, as the classic wheels do.
- **What it reads** (`scripts/install-cycle-assets.py` puts the Blender pack there): `models/rcl/lightcycle.rclmesh` (`scripts/convert-cycle-mesh.py`, from the .glb files with Blender's tangents); per level of detail `textures/rcl/lightcycle_lod<N>_{normal,basecolor,ao,roughness,mask}.png` (each level has its own UVs); `env_{pale,night}_spec_m0..5.hdr` and `_irradiance.hdr` (RGBE strips of six GL faces, GGX-prefiltered, roughness = level / 5); `brdf_lut_ggx.png` (16 bits).
- **Shading:** metal and roughness, the arena look's cubemap for reflections and its irradiance for the rest through the split-sum table, a clear coat where the mask says gloss, the lights in the bike's colour (`sg_BikeColor`, so two-tone holds) times `RCL_GLOW_CYCLE` into the glow. Where a map is missing, each material has its own colour, roughness and metal; without a look's cubemaps a sky and floor from the arena's colours and the sun as a light (GGX, Smith, Schlick); without the table Karis's fit of it. The cubemaps were baked with the game's sun, so the reflections fall the way the shadows do; `RCL_CYCLE_SUN` adds the sun as a light on top.
- **Levels of detail** by its length on the screen: `RCL_CYCLE_LOD1` 240 px, `RCL_CYCLE_LOD2` 80, under `RCL_CYCLE_FAR` 24 the light cycle with its far-away colour. The floor's mirror draws the mesh mirrored and shades the real cycle as seen from under the floor. The colour studio shows it in both arenas.
- **When it is not drawn:** with `RCL_RENDER 0`, outside the new renderer's scenes, without the mesh file, or once its shader has failed (for the session, with a console line), the light cycle (model 1) is drawn.
- `RCL_CYCLE_ENV 0` and `RCL_CYCLE_MAPS 0` leave the cubemaps or the maps out, to see what each brings. `RCL_SHADER_RELOAD 1` also reads the mesh, maps and cubemaps again when they change or appear.
- The GPU timers have a `cycles` pass, inside the world, which no longer counts the meshes.

- **The pack's lod1 and lod2 maps were left out:** baked at 21:42–21:45, they fit only 30% of the meshes exported at 21:48 (their material id map sampled at each triangle's middle; lod0's fit 88%). `install-cycle-assets.py` now makes that check and leaves a level's maps out until they are baked again on its mesh; those levels draw with their materials' own values.

Checked: it compiles on Windows, as the dedicated server, without GLEW and on the macOS code path; the shaders pass `glslangValidator`; the converter on the D2 .glb files (lod0 13544 vertices, 17515 triangles; lod1 4380; lod2 675); the mesh file against the loader's checks; the maps' orientation against the material id map. Not checked: nothing of it has been drawn yet.

Also, from the lead's measurements of P2 (1440p, 12 bots: GPU 0.16 ms classic, 0.50 new; CPU +0.25 to 0.4 ms a frame):

- `RCL_TONEMAP` defaults to 2 (see above).
- `RCL_HDR_FORMAT`: 0 picks R11G11B10F for the glow's chain and RGBA16F for scenes, which keep alpha for the clean arena's shadows (gathered there, also in the menu replay and the colour studio; without alpha they double up); 1 RGBA16F throughout; 2 packed floats for scenes too.
- CPU: the bound framebuffer is remembered instead of asked for; the composite saves only what it changes; uniform lookups are cached by the name's address; the mesh asks for the projection once a scene, saves less state and sets the scene's uniforms once. Queries were already never waited on, and nothing checks errors or completeness per frame. What is left to measure: the trail tops' pass (`RCL_GLOW_TRAIL 1` turns it off; one push and two program switches per cycle) and the glow (`RCL_BLOOM 0`).
- The glowing disc on the night floor is a death explosion: forty rays from where the cycle died, mostly low, growing to 100 units in a second and fading over two, drawn emissive at `RCL_GLOW_EXPLOSION` 4. Where they meet the glow adds up into a bright core, and the bloom spreads it into a soft ellipse. There is no filled disc; it fades with the explosion. `RCL_GLOW_EXPLOSION` 2 halves it, 1 turns it off.

## 2026-10-19 - render-next: GPU timers, the scene in a framebuffer, glow and Blender's view transform

Branch `rcl/render-next`, phases P0 to P2 of `rcl-cfg/rendering/EXPERIMENTAL_BRANCH_SPEC.md`. Jamie: "lets go for a build of this ingame given what we have discussed".

- **P0, measuring.** GPU timer queries per pass (`rGPUTimer`): world (every 3D scene), resolve, bloom, tonemap and other (the clear, HUD, console, menus), which add up to the frame. Only made while someone looks: `RCL_RENDER_STATS 1` shows them averaged twice a second at the bottom left with the CPU frame time; `RCL_FRAME_LOG` lines get `gpu world=... resolve=... bloom=... tonemap=... other=... lag=N path=P` after the old five fields (the newest frame whose answers are in, N frames back; path 1 the new renderer). They work with `RCL_RENDER 0` too, for the baseline.
- **The studio camera.** `RCL_STUDIO_CAMERA [file]`, or C in the colour studio, writes the preview's camera to `var/studio-camera.json`: eye, target and up in the light cycle's own frame (x ahead, y left, z up, the frame the Blender model is built in), vertical field of view, picture size, and the same in the arena. `RCL_STUDIO_HOLD 1` stops the cycle turning, at `RCL_STUDIO_SWING` degrees (0 facing the camera). `scripts/blender-studio-camera.py` sets a Blender camera from the file.
- **P1, the GL layer.** GLEW on Windows and Linux (configure finds it through pkg-config; without it the client builds and keeps the classic renderer); on macOS the 2.1 framework's own entry points (EXT framebuffers, GLSL 1.20). `rGLExt` (what the driver has), `rShader` (programs from files in `shaders/`), `rFramebuffer`, `rPost`. With `RCL_RENDER 1` (the default) each scene, the world of a viewport, the menu's replay and the colour studio's preview, is drawn into a framebuffer the size of the screen, multisampled with `RCL_ANTIALIAS` and resolved, and put on the screen in its viewport. HUD, console, scores and menus are drawn after it as before. `RCL_HDR 0` keeps that picture in 8 bits and copies it as it is: the classic picture, for a pixel comparison.
- **The way back.** If the driver lacks shaders or framebuffers, a shader does not compile or link, or no framebuffer can be made, the classic renderer draws for the rest of the session and the console says why (`render: the new renderer is off for this session ...`). `RCL_RENDER_FAIL 1` does that on purpose. Float colour or multisampling that cannot be had are given up first, with a console line, before the whole path is.
- **P2, glow and view transform.** The scene in half floats (`RCL_HDR 1`). The tone map takes its display values to light (power 2.2), adds the glow, applies `RCL_EXPOSURE` (stops) and the view transform `RCL_TONEMAP`: 2 (the default) the game's own colours with only what goes over white rolled off, 1 AgX, 0 none (clipped). AgX was the default at first; it turned a #ffff88 trail tan and greyed the pale arena, and trail colours tell players apart. A profile that ran the earlier build keeps the 1 it saved. The glow (`RCL_BLOOM`, `RCL_BLOOM_STRENGTH` 1, `RCL_BLOOM_THRESHOLD` 1 in light) is a dual filter (Bjørge 2015) from half size down five levels and back up.
- **AgX is Blender's own.** The published "minimal AgX" polynomial is the older AgX: against Blender 5.2's view transform (OCIO, display sRGB, look None) it was up to 60 8-bit steps off on saturated primaries (pure blue went violet) and 14 in the shadows. So `scripts/bake-agx-lut.py` bakes Blender's transform into `shaders/agx.cube` (33 cube, log2 shaper over AgX's own range), read by the tone map: half a step off on average, 3 for one colour in a hundred, up to 10 where one channel is nearly nothing beside a strong one. Without the file the polynomial stands in. AgX darkens and greys the pale arena: its floor, display 0.86, comes out at 0.72.
- **What gives off light** goes over 1 through `rEmissive` (the `emissive` shader; intensity in linear light like Blender's emission strength), only in a float scene: a line along the top of every clean wall and its rising end (a pass of its own, cached like the shadows), the classic glow shell, the clean zones' dashes and the classic zone walls, the light cycle's lights (`RCL_CYCLE_MODEL 1`; the arrow has none), explosions and the menu replay's death ring and wall lines. `RCL_GLOW_TRAIL` 3, `RCL_GLOW_ZONE` 2.5, `RCL_GLOW_CYCLE` 3, `RCL_GLOW_EXPLOSION` 4; 1 or less turns each off.
- **Names over the cycles** are written over the scene once it is on the screen, not into it, so the view transform does not grey them.
- Shaders are read from the data folder's `shaders/`, packaged on all three platforms; `RCL_SHADER_RELOAD 1` reads changed ones again about once a second, keeping the last good one when an edit does not compile.

Checked: the Windows client builds and links `glew32.dll`; every touched file compiles as the dedicated server compiles it, and the new render files compile on the macOS code path (no GLEW, entry points from the GL headers) and without GLEW, both through stand-in headers on Windows; the six shaders pass `glslangValidator`; the AgX table against Blender's OCIO as above. Not checked: nothing of it has run. No picture, no timing, no fallback has been seen; Jamie and the lead run every visual and performance test. Not built: Linux, macOS, the dedicated server.

## 2026-10-18 - a colour studio, the two-tone bike everyone else sees, a light cycle to try; the player page crash

Jamie: "we need a better colour studio both online in the tools and ingame now we have ingame we can do some fun stuff. we want to allow different colour bikes and trails and potentially expand into way more stuff in the future", with a concept image of a sleek light cycle ("like my screenshot").

- **How colours reach other clients.** A player sends one setting, `COLOR_R/G/B_1`. Every client shows the trail at min(255, 17 * value) in each channel, and tints the classic bike at (17 * value) mod 256, because the texture tint wraps. So a channel set above 15 gives a full trail with a different bike: 40/15/8 is a #a8ff88 bike with a #ffff88 trail. In a team of two or more the game clamps to 15 and blends the team colour in.
- **The clean arena showed neither.** `sr_CleanPaint` divided an overflowing colour by its brightest channel, so 40/15/8 came out one orange for bike and trail alike. It now clamps each channel, as GL does in every other client. The bike goes through `sg_BikeColor` (the classic tint's wrap), so the clean arena shows the two colours the others see.
- **Colour studio** (Settings, under Player; `gColourStudio.cpp`).
  - The bike and trail are chosen directly. The trail is 0–15 a channel or full; the bike is free in the channels where the trail is full, and otherwise follows the trail.
  - The setting is worked out per channel: trail below full gives v = trail step; trail full gives v = 241 * bike mod 256, raised by 256 if under 15, because 17 * 241 = 4097, which is 1 mod 256.
  - It shows the twelve pairs the website offers.
  - It has a live preview drawn by the game itself, turning: 1 light arena, 2 dark arena, 3 the classic bike with its textures.
  - Enter saves; Escape drops changes.
- **A light cycle in the clean arena, opt-in** (`RCL_CYCLE_MODEL 1`; the arrow stays the default until Jamie has seen it; `gLightCycle.cpp`).
  - The classic bike's layout, with flat facets and straight edges in dark graphite.

- **The menu replay** leaves its walls 0.9 behind each cycle, as the game does, not from its head.

The player object made as the player page closed then waited on a server for an id and ended the game with "Not enough nNetObject IDs to distribute". ## 2026-10-12 - the match behind the menus, played back right; a server browser of cards; soft shadows and a one-layer floor mirror

Four things from Jamie's play of the builds of 9 October, each in his words.

**The match behind the menus.** "the menus are still quite boring and the animation that plays is quite buggy. can you update it with actual physics and put the fort zones in the right places".

- What was buggy, measured on the replay file itself: from one stretch of a cycle's path to the next its speed changed by 12.6% in the median and 73% at the 99th percentile. The match log's positions are exact (a turn is logged at its corner; 99.3% of sample pairs lie along the first one's heading) but its times are not: a turn's line carries the turn's place and a clock a little behind it, and the old generator took the clock at its word. A cycle lunged into every corner and crawled out of it.
- `scripts/generate-menu-replay.py` now puts time back along the path from the speed the log gives with every sample (time = distance / speed), holds it to the log's clock only over two seconds, and keeps the log's own spacing where a cycle stands against a wall. Same match, same check: 3.1% in the median, 6.9% at the 90th percentile; a turn takes 5% by the servers' settings. File format 2: also each life's death, the servers' wall length and how long walls stand after a death (400 and 8 s), and when each zone fell (`fall`, from the log's conquests: all eleven have an attacker inside the zone that falls).
- The zones had been put at the mean of each team's starting places (near y 115 and 385). They are where the map has them, `Z-Man/fortress/for_old_clients-0.1.0`: (250, 50) and (250, 450), radius 40. A zone turns faster while attackers are in it and closes over two seconds when it was conquered.

## 2026-10-05 - a stall at every spawn, the round lag log, and a pass over buffers, caches and cleanup

**What the lag most likely was** (`gCycle.cpp`, `gTextureCycle`). Every cycle made its body and wheel textures when it was created: a copy of the image, a recolouring pixel by pixel and a mip chain built on the CPU, of a 2048x2048 and a 1024x1024 image (256 and 64 before the new cycle body of 2026-10-04), on the main thread. While it stands still it reads no network and game time runs on, so every sync read afterwards is that much older, and a cycle's lag-o-meter is drawn from exactly that (set straight to the age of a sync that carries a turn). The frame logs of the earlier online test runs had it all along: in the last one before this, twelve frames over 80 ms just before a round's start, 7.0 s between them over nine round changes, the longest 1.43 s. **Round lag log** (`gGame.cpp`, `gRclLagLog`; hooks in `nNetwork.cpp`, `gCycle.cpp`, `ePlayer.cpp`). For each online round, while a local player is alive (that is when lag costs something, and when the server talks to us often enough for its silence to mean anything):

- ping: the round's average, and the longest any one message waited for its acknowledgement (`nPingAverager::TakePeak`), less the frame it was read in;
- freezes: how often the server was silent for 0.3 s or more (and three times its usual gap), and the longest silence, counted from no earlier than the start of the stretch being measured;
- late syncs: how much older a cycle's sync was than that cycle's best in the round (the server keeps every cycle as far behind as its owner's lag, so the age alone says nothing);
- messages sent and sent again; other players whose ping as the server reports it moved by a tenth of a second; frames of our own over a tenth of a second, which are this machine and not the line, and are left out of the figures above.

A round is "lag" with a freeze, with late syncs over a quarter second, or with two resends and a peak well over the ping. One line per round goes to `var/rcl-lag.log` (tab separated; it starts over at half a megabyte and keeps the one before), a round left in the middle or lost with the connection included. - Cycle body: `scripts/generate-rcl-cycle-body.py` writes `models/cycle_body.mod` (lofted shell, 1122 vertices, replacing the 22-vertex body) and `textures/cycle_body.png` (player-coloured shell, pale fairing, canopy, headlight). `scripts/generate-rcl-textures.py` still has the old body texture as a source and would overwrite it. Cycle lights are neutral instead of red and blue.
- Text over the bright scene: bright text gets a dark drop shadow, the console plate is darker, and the HUD sits on a dark strip. HUD defaults drop the position, fastest and ping readouts.
- Menu replay drawn in the same look. Detail menu gains Anti-aliasing and Clean Arena.
- The "Turn Angle" and "deleting cycle" console lines are `#ifdef DEBUG` only; release builds, which CI now packages, do not print them.
## 2026-10-04 - map downloads on Windows, glow cache, anti-aliasing, floor reflections

- Map downloads: `tResourceManager` used libxml2's nanohttp, which MSYS2's libxml2 2.15 no longer has, so every map fetch failed on the Windows client. The plain HTTP client moved to `tools/tHttp` and resource downloads use it when `LIBXML_HTTP_ENABLED` is not defined.
- Trail glow: the glow of finished walls is cached in a display list next to the core wall list and rebuilt with it, instead of redrawing every segment in immediate mode each frame.
- Anti-aliasing: `RCL_ANTIALIAS` (samples per pixel, default 4, 0 = off) requests a multisampled GL visual and falls back to a plain one if the driver has none.
- Floor reflections: `FLOOR_MIRROR` defaults to cycles and walls on hardware renderers for new profiles. Existing profiles keep their setting.
## 2026-10-02 - menu replay, one menu style, Windows sign-in

- Menus: one panel style everywhere, palette taken from retrocyclesleague.com (neutral greys, `#e8ff47` accent) and kept in one place in `uRclTheme.cpp`. Menu copy is lowercased at draw time; text being edited is not. The label/value rule is only drawn on menus that have values. Help text wraps instead of being squeezed.
- Main menu: Play Now, Queue Now, RCL Account, Servers, Local Game, Player, Settings, About, Exit. Extras and Config Files moved under Settings.

Regenerate the file with `scripts/generate-menu-replay.py`; it writes no names, chat or ids.
- Sign-in on Windows: MSYS2's libxml2 2.15 has no HTTP client, so `nKrawall::FetchURL` always failed and RCL sign-in reported "unavailable". Without `LIBXML_HTTP_ENABLED` it now does the plain HTTP GET itself with a 6 s budget. Builds whose libxml2 has HTTP are unchanged.
